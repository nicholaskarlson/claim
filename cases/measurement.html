Check who was selected

Start with 500 paired no-offer respondents. Score each occasion by reversing stress item four and averaging at least four keyed answers. Rank the paired records by baseline score and then by student ID. Each ranked quarter has 125 records. The upper quarter changes by −0.426 points, the lower quarter by +0.341 and the full paired group by −0.023.

If you select at entry from all 608 no-offer respondents with a usable baseline score, the upper group has 152 students and 120 usable follow-up scores. Write the denominator you would use to describe coverage in that group. How would you state the change without attributing it to outreach?

Test a measurement assumption

The Pearson correlation of the 922 complete baseline score pairs is −0.363. Under an additive error model with stipulated reliabilities 0.70 and 0.85, the implied correlation is −0.402 with error correlation −0.25, −0.471 with zero error correlation and −0.539 with error correlation +0.25. At reliabilities 0.10 and 0.10 with zero error correlation, the calculation gives −3.631. Explain why this last row is infeasible. What evidence would justify choosing a feasible row?

To check the zero-error-correlation row, divide the observed correlation by the square root of the reliability product. For the other rows, first subtract the error correlation times the square root of the product of the two error variance fractions. Rounding the starting correlation can change the last digit.
